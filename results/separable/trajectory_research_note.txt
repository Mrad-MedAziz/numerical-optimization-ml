Research note for the eventual report
Recorded: 22 November 2026

Experiment
100 strictly separable 2D observations, NumPy seed 42; labels in {-1,+1};
no intercept, no regularization; mean logistic loss; full-batch gradient
descent initialized at zero, fixed learning rate 0.1, 100000 updates.
Independent reference: homogeneous hard-margin SVM solved with Gurobi.

Observation and investigation
The normalized weights first approached the SVM direction, then moved
away over a later part of the finite run. We examined each observation's
gradient contribution at the final weights, after 100000 updates.
Each contribution was divided by the dataset size, matching the mean loss.

We split the gradient into contributions from SVM support vectors and
from the remaining observations. A natural hypothesis was that the support
vectors would turn the weights toward the SVM direction. At these weights,
however, a hypothetical update using only their contributions increased
directional error. An update using only the other observations decreased it.
These diagnostic updates were evaluated separately at the same weights;
they were not separate training runs.

Support gradient: approximately (-0.0000685110, -0.0038458517)
Other gradient: approximately (-0.00100167, +0.00383373)
Total gradient: approximately (-0.0010701786, -0.0000121208)

Directional error before a hypothetical step: 0.010967378518756407
After a support-only step of size 0.1: 0.01098029972361371
After an other-only step of size 0.1: 0.010954475444092844
After the combined step: 0.010967396610984913

The groups' effects on the second weight nearly cancelled. Their combined
update still increased the second weight slightly and increased directional
error. This explains the local movement at that iteration, not the entire
trajectory or an eventual reversal. We compare normalized directions,
not raw weights. No monotone directional convergence is claimed.

Point 88 has positive label and positive second feature. Holding the first
weight fixed, increasing the second weight raises its signed score and
reduces its individual logistic loss. Its gradient has a negative second
component, so its negative-gradient update has a positive second component.
The other support point opposes this effect; at this iterate it is weaker.

Student-led diagnostic question
The student questioned whether selecting support vectors using np.isclose
could introduce tolerance artifacts. Testing tolerances from 1e-4 through
1e-14 selected the same two indices (49 and 88). Scores were 1 and
1.0000000000000007, both had positive solver multipliers, and the smallest
score outside the selection was approximately 1.37763. These numerical
checks supported the selection. See support_vector_checks.txt.
